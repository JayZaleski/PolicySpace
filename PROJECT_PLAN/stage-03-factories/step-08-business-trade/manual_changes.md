# Step 08: business-trade — Manual Changes

Businesses now act. Every turn, each business does what its decider decided: it sells and buys on its hex's market alongside the POPs, then runs and maintains its factories, and what they make is sold the next turn. The turn line gains four business totals, and the sample's chain (farm, mine, toolmaker) can be watched running in `check --turns` and the developer UI's turn log. The setting format and the sample setting don't change. This builds on the drafts of Steps 02, 03, 06 and 07 and makes several of their "not yet" statements out of date (see "Changes to earlier drafts").

## What users can now see or do

- **Businesses trade in the hex's market**, in the same round as the POPs. Every sale on a hex (POPs and businesses) happens before any purchase. A business sells what it offered, or everything it holds of that good if it offered more. It buys what it bid for at the posted prices, unless that costs more than the cash it has after its sales; then **every purchase is cut by the same proportion** (for example, a business with 10 silver bidding for goods worth 50 buys a fifth of each), so it gets a smaller set of goods in the proportions it asked for.
- **Then its factories run, wear and are maintained**, in that order, using goods from the business's stockpile. If a cash cut left the business short of an input or of maintenance goods, its factories run (or are maintained) less, in proportion to the shortage. The engine doesn't change the run levels the decider asked for; the shortage does.
- **Output is sold the next turn.** What factories make goes into the business's stockpile and is offered at the next turn's decision. So at turn 0 no business has anything of its own to sell, and from turn 1 on each sells last turn's output. Labor a business buys is used the same turn; anything left of it perishes at the end of the turn like any labor.
- **A business that decides nothing does nothing, but its factories still wear.** That covers a business whose decision was refused, one whose decider failed this turn, and every business in the Rust tests that run without Python. Its factories lose one turn's worth of wear every turn and eventually shut down. This is the first time stepping a world changes a factory.
- **A business on a hex with no market** trades nothing and runs its factories from what it holds (its deposits' output and starting inventory).
- **The turn line has four new parts**, after `population`: `businesses sold`, `businesses bought`, `produced` (what factories made) and `used` (the inputs and maintenance goods factories consumed), each a list in good order or `nothing`. They are kept apart from the POPs' `sold`, `bought` and `consumed`, so the POP numbers keep their meaning. They appear in `check --turns` and in the developer UI's turn log. A setting without businesses ends every line with `; businesses sold nothing; businesses bought nothing; produced nothing; used nothing`.
- **The sample's lines change** under its `arithmetic` decider. The POP parts are unchanged. Turn 0 shows the businesses buying (17.25 labor, 4 unimproved grain, 8 copper, 1.375 tools) and producing (2.5 copper, 12 grain, 4 tools), with nothing sold yet. From turn 1 they sell 2.5 copper, 12 grain and 3.875 tools each turn, and every turn repeats. `perished` from turn 1 is now only the unowned deposits' output (7.5 unimproved copper and 16 unimproved grain, because the factories use what the owned deposits produce), plus a tiny amount of labor printed in full (`0.0000000000000002220446049250313 unskilled_labor`). That sliver is a floating-point leftover from the arithmetic rule's bids for the toolmaker (bug 01 in `PROJECT_PLAN/bugs.md`). The manual can mention it briefly so readers aren't puzzled, and should say the engine rounds nothing.
- **Each business ends up richer every turn** in the sample: its cash after trading goes from 85 to 94 (farm), 37 to 43.5 (mine) and 152.25 to 152.5 (toolmaker) between turns 0 and 1, and every factory stays at full condition (the mine's, which starts worn at 0.75, is fully restored at turn 0).
- **Not visible yet:** a business's stockpile, cash, decision and per-factory results can't be inspected in the window (Step 09). Only the totals on the turn line show what businesses did.

## Changes to earlier drafts

- **Step 02 (businesses):** "Running a setting" was to say that businesses don't act in the middle part of the turn yet, and "Turn reports" that the sample's lines are unchanged by the businesses. Both are now wrong: businesses trade and operate in part 2, and the sample's lines gain the business parts and a different `perished`.
- **Step 03 (factories):** "Running a setting" was to say that the world holds factories but nothing in the turn uses them. Now factories run, wear and are maintained every turn, and their condition changes. The Factories subsection's description of condition should say it changes during play.
- **Step 04 (factory-mechanics):** its note for the Technologies subsection ("what the numbers will mean", "nothing runs yet") should now be in the present tense: the shutdown threshold, exact lifetimes and the run-wear-maintain order are what happens in a running world.
- **Step 06 (business-script) and Step 07 (decider-host):** "How the arithmetic rule decides" was to be presented as how businesses *will* behave, with decisions having no effect. It is now how they behave. The rule's cash budget is the normal path; the engine's proportional cut is a safety net under it. Step 07's note that the turn reports and the sample's output are unchanged is out of date.

## Where it goes

- **"Running a setting"**: rewrite part 2 of the turn as: decide, then on every hex trade (all sales, then all purchases, POPs and businesses alike), POPs consume, businesses run and maintain their factories; then POPs grow. Mention that the world is observed after this, so a freshly loaded world's factories have already run (or, for an idle business, worn) once. Then add a new subsection **"How businesses trade and produce"** after "How POPs trade" (and before "How a POP's size changes"), with the bullets above in plain terms: offers and bids, the proportional cash cut, operating the factories, output sold next turn, idle businesses wearing down, no market. Add it to the Contents list. Link [docs/trade.md](docs/trade.md) (the round, the cash cap and operation) and [docs/production.md](docs/production.md) (running, wear and maintenance).
- **"Turn reports"**: add the four business lists to the description of the report, say they are separate from the POP lists, and explain the sample's turn 0 versus turn 1 (nothing sold at turn 0), the smaller `perished`, and the labor sliver. The existing executed cell (`check settings/sample --turns 2`) needs no code change: it prints the new lines automatically, but the text around it must match them. That cell now starts the Python decider, so it needs the project's virtual environment (it already prints the checker's error if it fails).
- **"Checking a setting"**: the `--turns` description lists the four new parts.
- **"The developer UI"**: under "The world panel", the Turn log bullet says the lines now include what businesses sold, bought, produced and used. "Not available yet" keeps (or gains) "inspecting businesses, their stockpiles, decisions and factories" (Step 09).
- **The "Businesses" and "Factories" subsections** under "Writing a setting" (Steps 02 and 03): a sentence each pointing to "How businesses trade and produce" for what happens in play, and, for setting authors, that a business needs cash (its hex's currency in its `inventory`) to buy what its deposits don't cover, and that a factory nobody maintains wears out in `lifetime` turns.
- Link [docs/world.md](docs/world.md#turnreport) for the report's fields and [docs/decider.md](docs/decider.md) for why asking for too much is allowed.

## Example

A small table of the sample at turn 0 would make the chain concrete, one row per business: what it holds at the start (the farm's 8 unimproved grain from its fields and 100 silver; the mine's 2.5 unimproved copper and 50 silver; the toolmaker's 200 silver), what it buys (the farm 4 unimproved grain, 7 labor and 0.5 tools; the mine 4 labor and 0.75 tools; the toolmaker 8 copper, 6.25 labor and 0.125 tools), what its factory makes (12 grain, 2.5 copper, 4 tools), and its cash after trading (85, 37, 152.25). A second short line or column for turn 1 shows the same businesses selling that output (12 grain, 2.5 copper, 3.875 tools) and ending with more cash (94, 43.5, 152.5).

A worked example of the cash cut would help setting authors: a business with 10 silver bids for 10 labor (at 1) and 4 tools (at 10), costing 50. Every bid is cut to a fifth: it buys 2 labor and 0.8 tools, spending all 10 silver. Its smelter, asked to run at full capacity, finds a fifth of the labor it needs and runs at a fifth, while the decision still shows the full run it asked for. These numbers are from the `business_trade` test setting and can be quoted as they are.

No diagram is needed beyond, optionally, a simple flow of one turn for a business (decide → sell → buy → run → wear → maintain → output held for next turn). Don't add a new executed cell: the existing `--turns 2` cell already shows the business lines, and another cell that starts Python would only add a dependency.
