# The world and the turn

A loaded setting only describes a world: its rules and what turn 0 looks like. A `World` (`crates/engine/src/world.rs`) is that setting running. It adds the clock, the POPs' and businesses' holdings and the goods that exist, and it can be stepped turn by turn. The turn also asks the businesses' deciders what to do, through a decider host that the caller passes to `World::new` and `World::step`; the world holds no decider between calls (see [decider-host.md](decider-host.md)). The supporting types are `Turn` (`turn.rs`) and `Stockpile` (`stockpile.rs`). None of these modules knows about files; `world` depends only on the public setting types `LoadedSetting`, `Manifest`, and `Rules`.

## What a World holds

`World::new(setting: LoadedSetting, host: &mut DeciderHost)` consumes the loaded setting and takes it apart:

| Field | Source | Changes? |
|---|---|---|
| `manifest`, `rules` | the setting | never (goods, resource types, POP types and technologies are fixed for the whole game) |
| `map: HexMap`, `deposits: Deposits` | the setting's `StartState` | not yet; they are world state because later Stages change them. Each deposit's `owner` is set by the loader and nothing changes it yet (see [businesses.md](businesses.md)) |
| `turn: Turn` | starts at `Turn::ZERO` | advances by one per step |
| `hex_currencies: HexCurrencies` | the setting's `StartState::currencies` | not yet; world state because a later Stage changes a hex's currency (see [currency.md](currency.md)) |
| `hex_prices: HexPrices` | the setting's `StartState::prices` | not yet; world state because the live market (Stage 04) changes prices (see [market.md](market.md)) |
| `hex_stockpiles: IdVec<HexKind, Stockpile>` | empty at creation | goods enter by emission from unowned deposits and leave by perishing; nothing in the turn trades them |
| `pops: Pops` | the setting's `StartState::pops` | every turn: each POP's size is set by growth after the trade round (see [growth.md](growth.md)); hexes and types never change |
| `pop_stockpiles: IdVec<PopKind, Stockpile>` | one empty stockpile per POP at creation | goods enter by endowment and purchase, and leave by sale, consumption and perishing (see [trade.md](trade.md)) |
| `pop_outcomes: IdVec<PopKind, PopOutcome>` | built by turn 0's trade round | rebuilt every turn by the trade round: each POP's demand, what it sold, bought, consumed and carried over, its subsistence index and growth rate, and its starting size (see [trade.md](trade.md)) |
| `businesses: Businesses` | the setting's `StartState::businesses` | not yet (see [businesses.md](businesses.md)) |
| `business_stockpiles: IdVec<BusinessKind, Stockpile>` | one per business at creation, filled from its starting inventory | goods enter by emission from the deposits it owns, by purchase in the trade round and as factory output, and leave by sale, as factory inputs and maintenance goods, and by perishing (see [trade.md](trade.md)) |
| `factories: Factories` | the setting's `StartState::factories` | every turn: each factory's condition is written back after its owner's operation (see [trade.md](trade.md#operating-the-factories)); technology and owner are state because later Stages change them (see [factories.md](factories.md)) |
| `business_answers: IdVec<BusinessKind, BusinessAnswer>` | `Nothing` for every business at creation | replaced at every decision point: each business's answer (nothing, a valid decision, or a refused decision with its errors; see [decider-host.md](decider-host.md#answers-and-the-report)) |
| `business_outcomes: IdVec<BusinessKind, BusinessOutcome>` | built by turn 0's trade round | rebuilt every turn: each business's `BusinessTrade` (what it offered, sold, bid and bought, the cash cap's proportion, its cash at three points) and one `FactoryRecord` per factory it owns (see [trade.md](trade.md#businesstrade)) |

The world owns the setting, so callers (the dev UI) have no lifetimes to manage. There is one stockpile of **unowned goods** per hex, in an `IdVec` indexed by `HexId`, one stockpile per POP, the goods that POP owns, in an `IdVec` indexed by `PopId`, and one stockpile per business, the goods and cash that business owns, in an `IdVec` indexed by `BusinessId` (see [ids.md](ids.md)). All use the same `Stockpile` type. An owned deposit's output goes to its owner's stockpile, an unowned deposit's to its hex's.

Public accessors are read-only: `turn()`, `manifest()`, `rules()`, `map()`, `deposits()`, `hex_currencies()`, `hex_currency(hex)`, `hex_prices()`, `market(hex)`, `hex_stockpile(hex)`, `pops()`, `pop_stockpile(pop)`, `pop_outcome(pop)`, `businesses()`, `business_stockpile(business)`, `factories()`, `business_view(business)`, `business_answer(business)` and `business_outcome(business)`.

- `market(hex)` returns the hex's `Market` view (its prices in the hex's currency and the exchange, at the current turn; see [market.md](market.md)); because it borrows the world, the world can't be stepped while a `Market` is alive.
- `pop_outcome(pop)` returns the POP's `PopOutcome` from the current turn's trade round (see [trade.md](trade.md)).
- `factories()` returns the `Factories`, whose `on_hex` and `owned_by` give the factories on a hex and those a business owns, in ID order.
- `business_view(business)` builds a business's view of its circumstances at the current turn, the data a decider is served (see [decider.md](decider.md#the-view)). It reads and changes nothing.
- `business_answer(business)` returns the business's `BusinessAnswer` from the current turn's decision point.
- `business_outcome(business)` returns the business's `BusinessOutcome` from the current turn: `trade()` gives its `BusinessTrade`, `factories()` the `(FactoryId, FactoryRecord)` of each factory it owns in factory ID order, and `factory(id)` one factory's record (None if the business doesn't own it). The outcome doesn't copy the decision, its trace or its errors; those are in `business_answer`.

`hex_currency`, `market` and `hex_stockpile` panic with `is not from this list` for a hex from another map, `pop_stockpile` and `pop_outcome` for a POP from another world's `Pops`, and `business_stockpile`, `business_view`, `business_answer` and `business_outcome` for a business from another world's `Businesses` (a caller bug). The turn reads prices through the trade round but changes neither currencies nor prices. Because the world is observed after operation, `business_view(business)` called between steps shows the business after this turn's trade and operation (this turn's output held, conditions after wear and maintenance), not what its decider was served at this turn's decision point; that is next turn's view, before next turn's emission.

## The turn

A turn has three parts:

1. **Start of turn:** every deposit emits its resource type's `output` of its `produces` good, into its owner's stockpile if a business owns it and into its hex's stockpile otherwise (see [Emission](#emission)). Then every POP receives its type's endowment times its size into its own stockpile (see [Endowment](#endowment)).
2. **Actors act:** the decision point, then on every hex the trade round, consumption and factory operation, then growth. At the decision point every business's view goes to the decider its setting entry names, through the host passed to `new` or `step`, and its answer is stored (see [Decision point](#decision-point) and [decider-host.md](decider-host.md#the-decision-point)). Then on every hex each POP submits its parameters and holdings and each business the offers and bids its valid decision resolves to (none for a business that decided nothing or was refused); the hex's market computes the POPs' demand and settles every sale before any purchase, POPs before businesses in each pass, with a business's bids scaled by one proportion when they cost more than its cash; and each POP consumes its utility goods. Right after the hex's round, each business on it runs, wears and maintains its factories at the fractions its decision gives (0 for an idle business, whose factories only wear), and the conditions are written back. Then, once every hex is done, every POP grows or shrinks by the rate its outcome records (see [Trade round and operation](#trade-round-and-operation), [Growth](#growth), [trade.md](trade.md) and [growth.md](growth.md)). Later Stages add the live market.
3. **End of turn:** every group that perishes at the end of this turn is removed from every hex stockpile, then from every POP stockpile, then from every business stockpile.

Two calls drive the clock, and both run a turn through one private function:

- `World::new(setting, &mut host) -> Result<(World, TurnReport), UnknownDecider>` first checks that the host holds every business's decider name (`host.check`), and fails, building nothing, if one is missing. Then it creates the world at turn 0 with empty hex and POP stockpiles, each business's stockpile filled from its starting inventory (each good in the group it would get if added in turn 0, `PerishesAt::for_good(perishes, Turn::ZERO)`), and every business's answer `Nothing`, and runs `run_turn` for turn 0. So a fresh world's deposits have emitted on top of that, its businesses have answered, traded and operated their factories, and its POPs have already been endowed, traded, consumed and grown. In particular, a fresh world's factories are not at the conditions the setting places them at: under the idle host each has worn one lifetime-slice. The report's `perished` is empty.
- `World::step(&mut self, &mut host) -> Result<TurnReport, StepError>` first checks that the clock can advance (`Turn::next`) and that the host holds every business's decider name, so a refused step changes nothing (sizes, conditions, outcomes, business stockpiles and answers included). Then it runs the end of the current turn (`perish`: hexes in ID order, then POPs in ID order, then businesses in ID order), advances the clock, and runs `run_turn` for the new turn with what perished. The host may be a different one from the last call (a rebuilt host, say), as long as it holds every name.
- `World::run_turn(&mut self, perished, &mut host) -> TurnReport` (private) calls `emit`, `endow`, `decide`, `trade` and `grow` in that order and builds the report from their results: `emitted` and `endowed` from the first two, `decisions` and `decider_failures` from `decide`, `sold`, `bought`, `consumed`, `business_sold`, `business_bought`, `produced` and `used` from the private `TradeTotals` that `trade` returns (`trade` also operates the factories), `population` from `grow`, and `turn` from the clock.

So between calls the world is always **after part 2**: this turn's deposit output is in the hex stockpiles (unowned deposits) and the business stockpiles (owned deposits, unless a factory has used it), every business's answer for this turn is stored, every business has traded and operated its factories (its stockpile holds this turn's factory output, to be sold next turn, and its factories' conditions are after this turn's wear and maintenance), this turn's POP trade is done, each POP's stockpile holds what the round left, each POP's size is its size after growth, and nothing has perished yet this turn. This is the state that tests, the checker, and the dev UI observe. If observation happened after the end of the turn, goods with `perishes_after = 1` would always look empty.

`StepError` has two variants: `ClockAtMaximum` (the clock is at `Turn::MAX`), unreachable in practice, and `UnknownDecider(UnknownDecider)` (a business names a decider the host doesn't hold, displayed as the `UnknownDecider`'s message). Either way the world is unchanged. It implements `Display` and `std::error::Error`.

### Emission

`World::emit` (private) walks `deposits.iter()` in deposit ID order. For each deposit it looks up the resource type, computes `PerishesAt::for_good(good.perishes, self.turn)`, picks the target stockpile (the owner's in `business_stockpiles` if `deposit.owner` is `Some`, otherwise the deposit's hex's in `hex_stockpiles`), adds the type's `output` to it, and records `(good, output)`. Each deposit adds its output once; there is no multiplication by a count, so the order of floating-point additions is fixed. Several deposits of one good emitting into one stockpile merge into one group. The private `totals_by_good` helper then sums the recorded amounts per good (with `Quantity::saturating_add`) and returns them in good ID order, leaving out zero totals; these become `TurnReport::emitted`, one total over every deposit whether owned or not.

The hex stockpile stays the home of goods nobody owns. Ownership is set by the loader and doesn't change during play yet (see [businesses.md](businesses.md)).

### Endowment

`World::endow` (private) runs after `emit` and walks `pops.iter()` in POP ID order. For each POP it looks up the POP type in `rules.pop_types`, and for each `Endowment { good, amount }` adds `amount.saturating_mul(pop.size)` to the POP's stockpile in the group `PerishesAt::for_good(good.perishes, self.turn)`, recording `(good, total)`. `totals_by_good` turns the records into `TurnReport::endowed`. A type with an empty endowment adds nothing, and a world with no POPs behaves exactly as before POPs existed.

Deposits emit before POPs are endowed so the order of floating-point additions is fixed and `emitted` is unchanged by POPs. `endow` borrows `self.rules` and `self.pops` while mutating `self.pop_stockpiles`; they are separate fields, and the trade round relies on the same split (see [Trade round and operation](#trade-round-and-operation)). See [pops.md](pops.md) for POPs themselves.

### Decision point

`World::decide(&mut self, &mut host)` (private) runs after `endow` and before `trade`. It calls the host's crate-private `decision_point(self)`, which builds each decider's request from `business_view`, exchanges it, and validates the reply (see [decider-host.md](decider-host.md#the-decision-point)). It replaces `business_answers` with the answers, counts them into a `DecisionCounts`, and returns the counts and the failures for the report. The decision point only reads the world, so the views see the stockpiles after emission and endowment, with last turn's factory output and the conditions left by last turn's operation. The trade round and operation then act on the stored answers (see [Trade round and operation](#trade-round-and-operation)).

### Trade round and operation

`World::trade` (private) runs after `decide`. For each hex in hex ID order that has POPs or businesses, it resolves each business's answer into a `BusinessSubmission` with `business_submission` (moving its stockpile out), builds the hex's `Market` from the world's fields, moves each POP's stockpile out into a `Submission` (`std::mem::take`), and calls `trade::hex_round` with both. It moves the stockpiles back and stores each `PopOutcome` by `PopId`. Then, for each business on the hex in ID order, `operate_business` runs `production::operate` on the business's factories with its decision's fractions and writes the conditions back with `Factories::set_condition`, and the business's `BusinessTrade` and factory records are stored as its `BusinessOutcome`. After the last hex it replaces `pop_outcomes` and `business_outcomes` and returns the totals through `totals_by_good` as a `TradeTotals` (a private struct of seven lists, each in good ID order: POP `sold`, `bought` and `consumed`; `business_sold` and `business_bought`; `produced`, the output of every factory record with output; and `used`, every record's run inputs and maintenance cost), which `run_turn` puts into the report. A refused trade can't happen; if one does, it panics with an `engine bug` message naming the hex and the POP or business. The loop reads `map`, `pops`, `businesses`, `rules`, `hex_currencies`, `hex_prices` and `business_answers` and writes `pop_stockpiles`, `business_stockpiles` and `factories`, as separate fields, so the borrows don't conflict. See [trade.md](trade.md) for the round, the cash cap and operation.

### Growth

`World::grow` (private) runs after `trade`. For each POP in ID order it reads the growth rate from the POP's `PopOutcome`, sets the size to `growth::grown_size(size, rate)` (`size × (1 + rate)`, saturating) with the crate-private `Pops::set_size`, and adds the new size to a running total, which becomes the report's `population`. It reads `pop_outcomes` and writes `pops`, which are separate fields, so the borrows don't conflict. The rate was computed by the trade round from the POP's demand and its type's growth parameters; see [growth.md](growth.md) for the index and the rate.

### Perishing

`World::perish` (private) calls `Stockpile::remove_perishing(self.turn)` on every hex stockpile in hex ID order, then on every POP stockpile in POP ID order, then on every business stockpile in business ID order, and passes all the removed amounts through `totals_by_good`. The totals, hexes, POPs and businesses together, become `TurnReport::perished`.

### Determinism

No `HashMap` or `HashSet` is used. Deposits emit in ID order, POPs are endowed in ID order, deciders are taken in name order and the businesses within a decider in ID order, the trade round runs on hexes in ID order, POPs in ID order within a hex and then businesses in ID order within each pass, goods in ID order within each trader, with every sale on a hex before any purchase, then POP consumption, then each business on the hex operating its factories in factory ID order, POPs grow in ID order, stockpiles perish hexes first, then POPs, then businesses, each in ID order, stockpiles are `BTreeMap`s, and every list is in good ID order, so the same setting stepped the same number of times with deciders that answer the same way gives identical worlds (POP sizes, business stockpiles, factory conditions and answers included), outcomes and reports. `World` itself is not `PartialEq`, but `Stockpile`, `PopOutcome`, `BusinessOutcome` and `TurnReport` are, which is how tests compare two runs.

## TurnReport

```rust
pub struct TurnReport {
    pub turn: Turn,                         // the turn that started
    pub perished: Vec<(GoodId, Quantity)>,  // removed at the end of the previous turn, hexes, POPs and businesses
    pub emitted: Vec<(GoodId, Quantity)>,   // emitted by deposits at the start of `turn`, owned or not
    pub endowed: Vec<(GoodId, Quantity)>,   // received by POPs as endowment at the start of `turn`
    pub sold: Vec<(GoodId, Quantity)>,      // sold by POPs in the trade round of `turn`
    pub bought: Vec<(GoodId, Quantity)>,    // bought by POPs in the trade round of `turn`
    pub consumed: Vec<(GoodId, Quantity)>,  // consumed by POPs after the trade round of `turn`
    pub population: Quantity,               // total size of every POP after growth in `turn`
    pub business_sold: Vec<(GoodId, Quantity)>,   // sold by businesses in the trade round of `turn`
    pub business_bought: Vec<(GoodId, Quantity)>, // bought by businesses in the trade round of `turn`
    pub produced: Vec<(GoodId, Quantity)>,        // output made by factories in `turn`
    pub used: Vec<(GoodId, Quantity)>,            // inputs and maintenance goods consumed by factories in `turn`
    pub decisions: DecisionCounts,          // businesses with a valid decision, refused, and nothing, at `turn`'s decision point
    pub decider_failures: Vec<ReportedFailure>, // every decider that failed at `turn`'s decision point, in name order
}
```

Each list has one total per good, summed over every hex (and, for `perished`, every POP and business stockpile; for `emitted`, every deposit, whichever stockpile it emits into; for `endowed`, `sold`, `bought` and `consumed`, every POP; for `business_sold` and `business_bought`, every business; for `produced` and `used`, every factory), in good ID order, and lists only amounts greater than zero. `emitted` and `endowed` are kept apart so deposit output and POP income can be told apart, and the business lists are kept apart from the POP lists so the POP numbers keep their meaning. `population` is a single total, `Quantity::ZERO` for a world with no POPs. There is no per-hex, per-POP or per-business breakdown in the report: what each holds is in its stockpile, what each POP traded and how it grew is in its `PopOutcome` (`World::pop_outcome`), what each business traded and its factories did is in its `BusinessOutcome` (`World::business_outcome`), and a POP's size is in `World::pops()`.

For the sample setting under the idle host (every engine test that isn't about deciders), turn 0 reports "perished nothing; emitted 10 `unimproved_copper` and 24 `unimproved_grain`; endowed 4100 `unskilled_labor`; sold 2878.6 `unskilled_labor`; bought 5 `copper` and 1426.8 `grain`; consumed 1221.4 `unskilled_labor`, 5 `copper` and 1426.8 `grain`; population 419.4466666666666", and every business list empty. Because the POPs grow, later turns endow, trade and consume more (each turn's `endowed` is 10 labor times the previous turn's `population`), and `perished` is the deposit output only, since nothing uses it: the POPs sell or consume all their labor in the round. Under the `arithmetic` decider (the checker and the dev UI) the POP lists are the same, the businesses trade and their factories run (see [Setting checker](#setting-checker---turns-n)), and `perished` from turn 1 is the unowned deposits' output only (7.5 `unimproved_copper`, 16 `unimproved_grain`, since the factories use what the owned deposits emit), plus a floating-point sliver of `unskilled_labor` that the toolmaker's purchases leave (see [trade.md](trade.md#slivers)).

`decisions` adds up to the number of businesses; `decider_failures` is empty when every decider answered (see [decider-host.md](decider-host.md#answers-and-the-report)). The turn line doesn't print `decisions`; failures are printed as lines of their own after it.

## Turn

`Turn` is a `u64` newtype (`Turn::ZERO`, `Turn::MAX`, `new`, `get`, `next`, `saturating_add`), `Ord`, `Default` (zero), and `Display` (the number). The type stops turn numbers being confused with other integers, and `next` returning `None` at `MAX` means the clock can never wrap. It derives serde's `Serialize` and `Deserialize` as a newtype, so in JSON a turn is a bare whole number; a fraction, a negative number or a string is refused (see [decider.md](decider.md#the-lossless-rule)).

## Stockpile

`Stockpile` is a general type, not specific to hexes. Hexes use it for unowned goods, POPs for what they own (see [pops.md](pops.md)), and businesses for their goods and cash (see [businesses.md](businesses.md)); a business's factories take their inputs from and put their output into its stockpile (see [production.md](production.md)). It is a `BTreeMap<GoodId, BTreeMap<PerishesAt, Quantity>>`: sparse (only goods with a positive amount, only groups with an amount greater than zero) and ordered (good ID order, then soonest group first).

Each good's amount is split into groups by when it perishes, so `perishes_after` works without tracking individual units:

```rust
pub enum PerishesAt { EndOfTurn(Turn), Never }   // derives Ord: EndOfTurn(t) by t, then Never
```

`PerishesAt::for_good(perishes, added_in)` gives `Never` for a durable good and `EndOfTurn(added_in + n - 1)` for `Perishes::After(n)` (saturating at `Turn::MAX`). That is the meaning of `perishes_after` fixed in [goods.md](goods.md): with `perishes_after = 1`, output emitted in turn 5 can be used in turn 5 and is gone when turn 5 ends; with 3 it is gone at the end of turn 7. A good with `perishes_after = 3` emitted at 5 per turn therefore holds 5, 10, 15 in turns 0 to 2, and 15 from then on, with 5 perishing and 5 emitted each step.

Reading (public):

| Method | Returns |
|---|---|
| `is_empty()` | true if it holds nothing |
| `iter()` | `(GoodId, Quantity)` totals, in good ID order |
| `total(good)` | the total of one good (zero if none) |
| `groups(good)` | `(PerishesAt, Quantity)`, soonest first (empty if none) |

Changing (`pub(crate)`, engine only):

- `add(good, amount, at)` merges `amount` into the group `at`; a zero amount changes nothing.
- `remove(good, amount) -> Result<(), Quantity>` takes `amount` out, **soonest-perishing group first**, then the next, so what would spoil first is used first and durable units are kept longest. It is all-or-nothing: if the stockpile holds less than `amount`, it returns `Err(held)` (the total held) and changes nothing; there is no partial removal. A zero amount is `Ok` and changes nothing. A group that reaches zero is dropped, and so is a good left with no groups, so the stockpile stays sparse. Because the check compares against the floating-point total of the groups, a tiny remainder from rounding can be left unremoved when the groups run out; that is accepted.
- `retain(good, keep) -> Quantity` removes everything of `good` above `keep`, soonest-perishing group first, and returns the amount removed (zero, changing nothing, if it holds `keep` or less). It walks the groups from the latest-perishing back, keeping whole groups while they fit in `keep`; the group where `keep` runs out is set to exactly the remainder (or dropped if that is zero), and every sooner group is dropped. So when the kept amount lies in one group, exactly `keep` remains, with no rounding sliver; `keep = 0` removes the good entirely (there is no separate "remove all" method).
- `remove_perishing(turn)` removes every group at `EndOfTurn(t)` with `t <= turn` (not only `t == turn`, so no group can be stranded), drops goods left with no groups, and returns the totals removed per good.

There is no public way to change a stockpile. Removal is used by the dummy market (see [market.md](market.md)), which is the only way code outside the engine can change one: a caller passes a stockpile it owns (in tests, a clone of a hex stockpile) to `Market::sell`, `sell_down_to`, `buy` or `buy_for`. Inside the engine, the trade round sells POP goods with `sell_down_to` (which uses `retain`), sells business offers with `sell`, buys with `buy` and `buy_for`, and removes consumed goods with `remove`; the factory mechanics remove inputs and maintenance goods with `remove` and add output with `add` (see [trade.md](trade.md) and [production.md](production.md)). A `GoodId` does not say which `Goods` list it came from, so a stockpile simply holds the IDs it is given; the world only gives it IDs from its own rules.

## Quantity

`Quantity`'s arithmetic is `saturating_add` and `saturating_mul`, which clamp at `f64::MAX` so the result of two finite values never becomes infinite; `checked_sub`, which returns `None` if the result would be negative and never returns `-0.0`; and `checked_div`, which returns `None` when dividing by zero and otherwise clamps at `f64::MAX` (zero divided by a positive value is zero). `Quantity::ONE` is one unit (the price of a hex's currency in its own market). Arithmetic is added only when a system needs it (see [resources.md](resources.md)); `checked_div` exists for Stone-Geary demand (see [demand.md](demand.md)). `quantity.rs` also defines the JSON form, in hand-written `Serialize` and `Deserialize` impls: a quantity is written as a plain number, the shortest text that reads back to the same `f64`, and read through `Quantity::new`, so a negative or non-finite value is refused. This is the one place the written form is defined (see [decider.md](decider.md#the-lossless-rule)).

## Setting checker: `--turns N`

See the table in [setting-loader.md](setting-loader.md). `dev-ui check <SETTING_FOLDER> --turns N` loads the setting, prints the success line, starts the setting's deciders, creates a `World`, prints turn 0's line, then steps `N` times, printing one line per turn:


The line lists `perished`, `emitted`, `endowed`, `sold`, `bought` and `consumed` in that order, then `population`, then the business lists `businesses sold`, `businesses bought`, `produced` and `used`. Entries are `<amount> <good name>` joined by `, ` in report order (good ID order: `unskilled_labor` comes first, `copper` before `grain`, and `tools` last in the sample's goods); an empty list prints `nothing`, so a setting without POPs or businesses ends every line with `; endowed nothing; sold nothing; bought nothing; consumed nothing; population 0; businesses sold nothing; businesses bought nothing; produced nothing; used nothing`. The amounts are floating-point sums printed with `Quantity`'s `Display`, with nothing rounded, so they can print with a long tail of digits, as the sample's do from turn 1 on once its POPs have grown, and slivers print in full (the `unskilled_labor` in turn 1's `perished`, and `0.8749999999999999 tools`). In the sample, nothing is sold by businesses at turn 0 because nothing has been made yet; from turn 1 each business sells last turn's output, and every turn after repeats turn 1's business lists. The line is built by `text::turn_line`. The checker is the headless `check` mode of the dev UI (see [dev-ui.md](dev-ui.md)). The dev UI window builds a `World` from a loaded setting, steps it with its Step buttons, and shows the same turn lines in its turn log as `check --turns` prints.

Before creating the world, `--turns` starts the setting's deciders with `python::host_for`: one `python -m policyspace <name>` process per decider name its businesses carry, with the project's virtual environment (see [decider-host.md](decider-host.md#finding-python-deciderpython)). A setting without businesses starts no Python. If a decider can't start (the environment is missing, or the Python package doesn't know the name), or a business names a decider the host doesn't hold, the checker prints the message and `1 error(s) found in <path>` to stderr after the success line, runs no turns, and exits with 1. A decider that fails during a turn is not an error: after that turn's line it prints `Turn {n}: decider '{name}' failed: {reason}`, then each line of the process's error output as `    stderr: {line}`, and carries on.
